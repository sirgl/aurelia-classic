# Re-sourcing the classic data

The project owner, 2026-10-21: the publication audit found that most of the classic data was assembled from sources that are not permitted ("this must definitely be fixed!"). The only permitted source is Unciv (<https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv>, MPL-2.0): its public JSON rule files and its Kotlin code for behaviour. Forbidden: the original game's XML tables, the game-core SDK sources, the game's database as dumped on a wiki, and every verbatim game text. The classic module stays private for now; its data must be clean before that changes.

This file is the working list. Row format: path | what | forbidden source | replacement plan | status. `classic/docs/classic/SOURCES.md` is the older inventory (it may name the forbidden sources because it has to); this file is the work plan and the result. Counts marked [measured] were taken on the branch `refactor/resource-classic` (base `c3988297`) by script or `git grep`.

## Fact that changes the plan: Unciv has no Brave New World rule set

Unciv's repository (commit `b6377fdb`, master on 2026-10-21, shallow clone) has exactly two Civ V rule sets: `Civ V - Vanilla` and `Civ V - Gods & Kings` (`android/assets/jsons/`). There is no BNW rule set [measured: directory listing; `grep -ril "brave new" android/assets/jsons` finds nothing]. So:

- Everything Unciv G&K has is re-sourced from Unciv (numbers, prerequisites, names).
- BNW-only content (the Internet technology, the three ideologies and 48 tenets, the Exploration branch, 14 beliefs, 12 unique units, 8 civilizations, trade routes, tourism, great works, World Congress, spies as BNW has them) has no Unciv source and no permitted source. Its numbers are own values ("own value" where checked by hand, "own value, unverified" otherwise; never kept as if they were sourced), its texts are dropped. The list of such entries is in the "Residual" section and is regenerated by the generator.
- The `edition` field says "Civ 5 inspired rule set (Unciv Gods & Kings ruleset plus own extensions)" and not "Unciv BNW ruleset", because no such Unciv ruleset exists.

## 2. Method

1. `classic/tools/import-unciv.py --unciv DIR` reads Unciv's `Civ V - Gods & Kings` JSON (the Vanilla set where G&K lacks a file), maps entry by entry (name match after dropping case, a leading "the", punctuation; the renames in `ALIASES`) and writes the catalogs `classic/docs/classic/*.json`: scalar rule fields come from Unciv; typed effects come from Unciv's `uniques` where the unique is in the translation table of the script and equals the effect we already model; every other field keeps its present value with the provenance `own value, unverified`. The output is committed; the script is reproducible (same Unciv commit gives byte-identical files).
2. `classic-import` turns the catalogs into `classic/assets/rules/classic/*.ron` as before.
3. Texts: names are facts and stay; descriptions are not copied. The UI describes policies and beliefs from their typed effects.
4. Attribution: every generated file starts with "Derived from Unciv (https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv), MPL-2.0" (JSON files carry it in the `notice` field), and `classic/docs/classic/UNCIV-ATTRIBUTION.md` lists each derived file with the Unciv file it came from.
5. Gameplay numbers that changed because Unciv's value differs from the old one are accepted (the project owner wants Unciv-like behaviour); each re-pin is its own commit with the reason.

## 3. Result and residual

Measured on the branch [measured by me, `python3 -I classic/tools/import-unciv.py --unciv DIR --report`]: of 1000 catalog entries, 697 have an Unciv counterpart and carry `provenance: unciv: <File>.json, <entry>`; 303 do not (Brave New World additions and our own extensions); they carried `provenance: own value, unverified` (now 207 of them `own value` after a hand check and 14 `unciv:`, see below). 150 of 387 effects of buildings and wonders were compared with Unciv's stats and uniques and are marked `unciv:`; the other 1099 typed effects of all catalogs were `own value, unverified` at that point (the passes below bring them to 272).

**Hand check (2026-10-21)** [measured by me, `import-unciv.py --report` and a count over the catalogs]: the entries without an Unciv counterpart were checked by hand; `classic/tools/own-overrides.json` records, per entry id, the values written and the effects marked checked; `import-unciv.py` applies it as the last step of its overlay (idempotent). 207 of the 303 entries carry `provenance: own value` (no external source is named); 322 typed effects carry `source: own value`, 150 `unciv:`. No external source remains for these numbers: they are our own design.

**Unique translation pass (2026-10-21)** [measured by me, `import-unciv.py --report --uq-log` and a count over the catalogs]: Unciv writes an entry's rules as unique texts, our catalogs as typed effects. `UNIQUE_RULES` in `import-unciv.py` translates 130 of Unciv's unique patterns (strength with its conditionals, sight/movement/range, healing, extra attacks, interception, cargo, yields per city/building/tile/specialist, production and growth percents, free units/techs/policies/buildings, religion, espionage, nation traits) into claims (modifier, amount, condition tokens, selector). Every effect still `own value, unverified` of an entry that cites Unciv is compared with the claims of that entry: equal modifier, condition and selector and equal amount -> `source: unciv: <File>.json, <entry>`; the same rule with another number -> Unciv's number is taken (Unciv wins where it has the rule). Copies of one rule that our catalogs split by a selector filter (land and sea units, two resources) share the confirmation. A promotion Unciv has no promotion for is compared with the uniques of the units that carry it (their uniques and their Unciv promotions) and of the wonder, policy or nation that grants it; when all its effects are confirmed it cites that Unciv entry. A building stat (culture, happiness, gold, percent bonus) that differs from Unciv's entry takes Unciv's number (`BUILDING_STAT_CORRECTIONS`; its own commit, so it can be dropped alone). Two mapping defects were fixed: a branch finisher is Unciv's "<Branch> Complete" (it matched the branch before), a branch opener maps only to a branch (the Brave New World Aesthetics branch had matched Unciv's Patronage policy of that name; it is checked by hand (own value) now).

Result: of 1213 typed effects, 597 cite Unciv (150 before), 344 `own value` after a hand check (322), 272 stay `own value, unverified` (741). Twelve of those belong to entries that cite Unciv but carry Brave New World effects (`effects_only` in the overrides file): the trade-route slot of eight techs and of Colossus and Petra, and their free trade unit. Promotions: 140 cite Unciv, 44 are `own value` after a hand check, 30 stay own value, unverified (82 before). Values taken from Unciv (old -> new): Louvre free Great Artists 1 -> 2, Theocracy temple gold +25% -> +10%, Religious Art Hermitage culture 5 -> 8, Supply heal in foreign land 5 -> 15, Survivalism III withdraw chance 50% -> 75%, Coastal Raider III plunder 34% -> 33%, paradrop range 9 -> 5, Marine embarked sight 2 -> 1, Cossack bonus vs wounded 33% -> 50%, Porcelain Tower and Scientific Revolution research agreement science +25% -> +50%, Great Firewall enemy spy effectiveness -100% -> -99%, Rationalism finisher free techs 1 -> 2; building stats: Stadium happiness 2 -> 4, culture of Opera House 1 -> 4, Museum 1 -> 5, Broadcast Tower 1 -> 3, Amphitheater 1 -> 3, Cathedral 1 -> 3, Hermitage 2 -> 5, Great Wall 1 -> 3, Himeji Castle 1 -> 3, Kremlin 1 -> 3, Louvre 2 -> 4, Brandenburg Gate 1 -> 3, Pentagon 1 -> 3, Terracotta Army 1 -> 6, Market gold 1 -> 2, Satrap's Court gold 3 -> 2, Stock Exchange gold +25% -> +33%. ### Kept differences from Unciv

- Prince AI unit upkeep percent: ADOPTED (100, as Unciv) in the formula pass. Two causes kept the twelve-tile conquest scenario from capturing the city at 100: (1) the Advance/Rally oscillation, fixed in the AI front code (a member on the gathering ring that cannot see its target holds and heals there; regression test `a_blind_front_member_holds_on_the_ring`); (2) the old unit upkeep curve (`(1 + 8p)`, exponent `1 + p/7`) drained the treasury from round 5, so the AI bought nothing and grew unhappy; Unciv's upkeep (3 free units, `(0.5 paid (1 + p))^(1 + p/3)`) was adopted in the same pass and the scenario now captures the city (`conquest_twelve_tiles_away` passes at 100). ## Infamy of a nuclear strike (2026-10-23, own design)

**Rule.** Using a nuclear weapon is a public act. When a `Nuke` command runs, every living major civilization that has met the user, and every owner of what the blast hit (units, cities, land), adds `WARMONGER_NUKE_WEIGHT` = 400 times the weapon's level (1 or 2), scaled by the game's era percent like a declaration of war, to its warmonger memory of the user (`systems::diplomacy::warmonger_for_nuke`). It applies to the human and the AI alike, whoever the target is and whatever the war between them; city-states and barbarians keep no such memory. No new state: the warmonger memory is the existing record (`world::relations`, directed observer -> player, saved, hashed, fading by `WARMONGER_DECAY` = -5 a round, scaled by the era), which the diplomacy screen already shows to the human ("You remember their warmongering"), so the save format does not change. For scale: a declaration of war weighs `WARMONGER_ATTACKED_WEIGHT` = 250, so one strike passes the AI's denunciation line (250) at once and takes about 80 rounds at the nuclear era's percent to fade.

**What the memory does** (all of it already existed, nothing parallel was added): the AI distrusts above 100 (`ai::diplomacy::trusted`: no friendship, pact, embassy value), denounces above 250, and counts the other's army as hostile in its danger estimates. New in this work, in the AI (`ai::infamy`, in the allocator's currency of hundredths of a score point): peace with a civilization whose memory is above the line of distrust costs `CENTI_PER_POINT` = 6 per point above it (the AI gives such a civilization peace only for compensation); a war operation against it is worth half of that more; and a nuclear strike costs its AI user `WARMONGER_NUKE_WEIGHT` x level x `CENTI_PER_POINT` for every civilization it has met and is not at war with (2,400 per witness at level 1: a city of six citizens is about 100 points). The AI therefore nukes only when the strike gains more than the world's memory costs; with four witnesses a strike that gains 7,071 stays in its silo (`tests/ai_strikes.rs`).

**Why these numbers** (own values, not tuned against a bench): the weight is above one declaration and below two so that a strike is a graver act than a war and still fades; the price per point is set so that the cost of a strike (about 24 points per witness) is about a sixth of a captured city's worth, which makes a nuclear strike a choice of last resort for the AI without forbidding it.

**Tests.** `classic-rules/tests/nuclear_infamy.rs` (the rule: witnesses, the victim, strangers, the level, fading, city-states and barbarians), `tests/ai_strikes.rs` (the AI's cost of witnesses, the price of peace with an aggressor), `ai::infamy` unit tests; the player is told of every strike by a notice (`classic.game.turn.nuclear-strike`, seven languages; `classic-ui` test `a_nuclear_strike_is_announced_to_the_player`).
